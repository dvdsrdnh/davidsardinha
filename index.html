A Designer at the End of Nowhere-Elsewhere

Based on the motto "A Designer at the End of…" we collectively reflect on what it means to design in a world marked by systemic crises, where the future seems canceled before it can even be imagined.

It is in this context that the project examines the tension between what a designer believes they can do and what reality imposes upon them; between the urgency of the present and the rhythm of daily work; between the academic narrative that portrays the designer as an agent capable of resolving the world's crises, and the performative morality that this narrative ultimately produces.

Nowhere-Elsewhere emerges as a psychogeographical territory, an antimap that arranges the projects adrift, guided by uncertainty as a productive condition. All that remains is to inhabit this non-place as a possible, and perhaps even desirable, condition.

( 2026 )

Afterpop

What if the sense of future shock in pop music has disappeared?

Afterpop reads contemporary pop through repetition rather than rupture. Taking the Brat era as a symptom of a present looping on itself, where novelty stalls and the future feels oddly familiar, it traces how pop has traded disruption for recurrence, and what that reveals about the cultural imagination of now.

Positioned between theory, pop culture, and visual practice, it treats contemporary culture not as a succession of trends but as a diagnostic tool, a space where broader political, technological, and economic conditions become visible.

Apagão

In celebration of Design Day, we organised an event that proposes a deliberate pause from design's productive logic, opening space for experimentation, error, and process.

Inspired by the stillness that follows an unexpected power outage — when everything stops and people simply breathe —,Apagão invites us to slow down, bringing together a conversation with Rick Rubin, Bráulio Amado and Yara Kono, and a hands-on workshop with João Pestana and Duarte da Costa.

Everything is Romantic, Even Design

Inspired by the Lenço dos Namorados, we wrote a love letter to design, embroidered onto a canopy, exploring the fragility and emotional persistence in the face of the designer's precarious profession. It rejects functional utility, embracing the act of embroidering as political and relational expression, not asking what design does, but what it can make us feel.
